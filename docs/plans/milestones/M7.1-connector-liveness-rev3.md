# M7.1 — connector liveness rev 3

**Status:** review draft; supersedes [rev 2](M7.1-connector-liveness-rev2.md).
[Rev 1](M7.1-connector-liveness-rev1.md) and rev 2 are preserved

**Planning base:** `83ee65d` (`main` after PRs #19, #20, and #21)

**Scope:** close the M7 record's open connector-liveness item by having
`panel()` state its membership in the IR primitive that already exists for it,
and by letting that primitive be used on a `many` port

**Authority:** `docs/INVARIANTS.md` → `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` → accepted ADRs →
code/tests → this draft

## 1. What rev 2 got wrong

Rev 2 scoped a `many` port's pool to its direct connections and layered
liveness on top. Cross-review found the exactness claim false and the scoping
unsafe in two places.

| Rev 2 said | Why it could not stand | Rev 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laws 1+2 make membership exact | A member drifting to a contract that binds a *different* input on the same aggregator satisfies liveness while the gather loses it | The claim is checked at the port, not the node |
| Scoping is safe for composites | An outer `many` boundary retags sources as inner graph inputs; an inner collector then has no direct predecessor and faults, with no `$input` map available inside | The pool is not touched |
| Loop bodies were measured | The synthetic `$body` node compiles outside `_compile_graph`'s traversal; the recorder never saw it | The pool is not touched |
| Direct outputs "plus" mapped sources | A mapped port *replaces* pool binding today; that is a different law | Mapped semantics are the law |
| `description_digest` unchanged | `describe()` advertises the `many` rule; changing the rule changes the digest | Stated, with the reason |

Rev 1's refutations stand and are not re-argued. Rev 2's measurement stands
too, and is still the reason this design is safe to make: every gather in the
repository already draws only directly-connected sources.

## 2. The defect, and the gap that produced four wrong laws

A member whose resolved output contract differs from the one the panel was
authored against is silently absent from the gather. Reproduced with
`sdk/panel-yes` resolving to a version emitting `sdk/Verdict` where the panel
expects `sdk/Vote`: admission succeeds, the gather holds one member where two
were connected, and nothing reports the absence. It needs no later promotion —
`panel()` emits unversioned refs, so a bundle can disagree with the current
stable on first admission.

Four laws were proposed across three revisions and each failed at the same
place. **A `Connection` targets a node. Membership is a claim about a port.**
Every law tried to infer port-level intent from node-level edges — was this
source bound *somewhere* at the destination? — and the gap between "somewhere"
and "here" is exactly where the cross-port counterexample lives.

The IR has had the port-level primitive since M1. `Connection.map` names a
destination port and the selector that fills it, and `_validate_explicit_sources`
already refuses a selector whose contract does not match. `panel()` does not use
it. It computes the aggregator's `many` port, proves it matches the members'
result, and then:

```python
del gathers  # proved present and matching; it is fed by the members, not the boundary
```

That is the whole defect in one line: the proof is completed at authoring and
discarded rather than written into the graph. What follows is the only change
this document proposes to what admission *checks* — none — and one change to
what `panel()` *says*.

## 3. The design

> **A. Several connections may map one `many` port.** When more than one
> connection into a node maps the same destination port, the port is bound to
> the union of their selectors, in connection order, each validated against
> the port's contract. A port of cardinality `one` or `optional` still admits
> exactly one distinct selector.
>
> **B. `panel()` maps every seat.** Each member connection carries
> `map={<gather port>: "<member>.<result port>"}`. Membership is authored, and
> admission re-proves it with the contract check that already exists.
>
> **C. A connection names two nodes.** A connection whose `src` or `dst` is
> not a node of the graph is refused rather than skipped.

A is a correction to `_explicit_maps`, which today refuses two different
selectors for one destination port regardless of cardinality — a rule written
for `one` and applied to `many` by omission. Nothing in the suite asserts on
that refusal. B is sugar. C is the endpoint law rev 1 found and rev 2 kept.

## 4. Why it holds where the others broke

**The pool is not touched.** `one`, `optional`, and unmapped `many` ports bind
exactly as they do today. The `issue-to-summary` chain, the ambiguity repair,
the conduit at a gather, composite boundaries, and Loop bodies are all
unaffected because none of them is mapped.

**The check is at the port.** A drifted member's selector names its result
port; `_validate_explicit_sources` compares that source to the gather port's
contract and refuses the mismatch. Whether the drifted output *also* binds some
other input on the aggregator is irrelevant — the claim on the gather port is
false, and that is what fails. The cross-port counterexample is closed by
construction.

**The fault already exists.** `GRAPH_PORT_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` is what
`_classify_fault` assigns to "explicit selector names […] but the port requires
[…]" (`runtime/authoring.py:438`). Its message names the member and the port's
contract. No new code, no new frame, no new schema.

**Aggregator drift is caught too.** If the aggregator's resolved version changes
its gather port's contract, every member selector mismatches at once. Rev 2
listed this as unaddressed.

**Counterfactual replay is correct by construction.** Under a `ResolutionLock`,
`_resolve_ref` takes the pinned version, so members resolve exactly as they did
in the source run and no selector can newly mismatch. An override that pins a
version with a different contract produces a mismatch that is *real*, and
`COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH`'s existing repair — "use a contract-compatible
exact override" — is precisely the right advice. That sentence was written for
this case.

**Exactness is opt-in, and `panel()` opts in.** A hand-authored gather without
maps keeps the transitive semantics `ARCHITECTURE.md` documents, and the
`sdk/wider` hazard remains available to anyone who wants it. A hand-author who
wants exactness maps the port. The measurement in rev 2 § 3 says nobody in this
repository relies on the transitive gather except the test that demonstrates
it, so the documented semantics are preserved at no cost and exactness is one
`map` away for everyone.

## 5. What it costs

- **A re-authored panel is a different graph.** Its connections carry maps, so
  `source_graph_hash` and therefore `manifest_hash` differ from the same panel
  authored before this change, and every effect idempotency key with them.
  Retained runs are unaffected: they never re-admit. This is the ordinary cost
  of changing what a combinator emits.
- **Bindings should be byte-identical, and that must be proved.** For a pure
  fan-in, closure order is connection order, so the unmapped pool and the mapped
  selectors should produce the same `ResolvedPortBinding.sources` in the same
  order. Rev 2's provenance counts do not establish ordering; a golden does.
- **`description_digest` changes.** `BindingVocabulary` advertises the
  resolution order for unmapped ports and the selector forms; A is a new
  statement about selectors and truthful discoverability requires publishing
  it. Whether that is a `SystemDescription` schema 2 is the ADR's to decide —
  the model is `extra="forbid"`, so a pre-change reader rejects the new field.
- **`sdk/wider` inverts.** Its bystander, graph-input, and helper cases stop
  widening a `panel()` gather, and the test becomes the proof of B rather than
  the documentation of a hazard.
- **One authoring check becomes false and is removed.** `panel()` refuses a
  boundary input carrying the members' result contract because it "would be
  gathered as a member". Under B it would not — a mapped port ignores the pool
  — so the check's stated reason is no longer true. A `one` policy input of
  that contract would still be caught by the existing ambiguity fault.
- **The duplicate-map fault moves.** `_explicit_maps` cannot know a port's
  cardinality; it runs before resolution. It accumulates selectors, and the
  arity check moves to `_bind_node_inputs` and `_compile_loop`, where the port
  is known. The message is unchanged.

## 6. What it does not close

- **An unmapped hand-authored gather is exactly as it was.** By design; § 4.
- **Nominal drift is still nominal.** A member whose contract changes while
  staying nominally identical is not visible to admission.
- **A map's selector may still name a node other than the connection's `src`.**
  Membership is exact at the port regardless of which connection carries the
  claim; the inconsistency is a smell, not a hole, and is left alone.
- **Runtime provenance is untouched.** The walker still drops source addresses
  when it collects a `many` port (`walker.py:1901`). That is the separate
  kernel-attestation item.
- **The fault names the member in prose, not in details.** Adding a framed
  frame to `GRAPH_PORT_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` would help an architect repair it and
  is a separate improvement.

## 7. Fault contract

**No new `AdmissionCode`, no new frame.** The code set is the versioned wire
schema and the M7 record already caught and reverted an additive code once.

| Event | Code | Already exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A mapped member's contract does not match the gather port | `graph.port.contract_mismatch` | yes |
| Two distinct selectors on a `one` or `optional` port | `system.admission.legacy` → the existing "maps destination port twice" message | yes, relocated |
| A connection names a node that does not exist | `graph.contract.invalid` with a framed `unknown_connection_node` | frame is new, code is not |

The endpoint frame carries an exact coordinate: a root `AdmissionFault.path`
for proposal-owned graphs, or `{component, version, definition_path,
connection_index}` for retained ones, because a fault raised inside a
referenced composite is classified against the root proposal
(`runtime/authoring.py:81`) and rendered scope is non-injective
(`runtime/authoring.py:480`). `missing` is a deterministically ordered list of
endpoint roles. A known-node self-connection is already a cycle fault; the
`src == dst` case here is the ghost one.

## 9. Required failure proof

**A — several maps on one `many` port**

- two connections mapping one `many` port bind the union of their selectors,
  in connection order;
- two distinct selectors on a `one` port still raise "maps destination port
  twice", now from the binding site;
- identical selectors on any port coalesce, as today;
- a Loop body's `many` input mapped from two connections binds both.

**B — `panel()` states membership**

- the § 2 drifted panel is refused with `graph.port.contract_mismatch`, and
  the message names the drifted member and the gather's contract;
- the same panel undrifted admits, and its `resolved_connections` are
  byte-identical to the pre-change golden — same sources, same order;
- a member drifting to a contract that binds a *different* aggregator input is
  refused — the cross-port counterexample, closed;
- an aggregator whose gather port's contract drifts is refused, naming every
  member;
- `sdk/wider`: a bystander, a compatible graph input, and a compatible helper
  upstream of a member are each **not** gathered by a `panel()` aggregator;
- the same three shapes on an unmapped hand-authored gather still are — the
  transitive semantics are preserved for the unmapped case;
- SDK and direct Graph forms remain byte-equal, the direct form now carrying
  the maps;
- counterfactual replay of a retained panel under its lock admits unchanged,
  and an override pinning a contract-incompatible member version reports
  `COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH` with its existing repair;
- one advisor round trip completes across a real process restart with the
  mapped graph — the e2e lane already builds its panels with `panel()`.

**C — endpoints**

- a ghost endpoint is refused at the root, in an inline nested Graph, in an
  inline Loop body, and in a nested Graph inside a retained composite, each
  with an exact coordinate;
- both endpoints unknown, and `src == dst` ghosts, yield one fault with an
  ordered `missing`;
- a ghost `src` whose map determines a binding is refused rather than applied;
- proved through `admit_graph()`'s public JSON surface and the exported
  `runtime.admit`, not only the authoring preflight;
- counterfactual replay of a retained graph carrying a ghost reports the
  connection fault through `ControlRejected`, not `COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH`.

**Across all**

- `issue-to-summary`, `architect-selection`, `mapped-loop`, `diamond`, and
  `gathering` each admit to a byte-identical manifest against a pre-change
  golden.

## 11. Implementation sequence

- **ADR.** "Several connections may map one `many` port, and `panel()` states
  its membership through them." It precedes or accompanies PR A and is
  accepted with it; implementation does not become authority before the
  decision is.
- **PR A — maps.** Design A and B, the `sdk/wider` inversion, the goldens, the
  vocabulary statement, and the authoring-check removal.
- **PR B — endpoints.** Design C, the exact-coordinate frame, and the
  `ControlRejected` projection for counterfactual.

## 12. Review decision requested

1. approve rev 3 and the ADR it requires;
2. approve with redlines, producing rev 4 while preserving this file;
3. reject with the violated invariant or the counterexample named.

The substantive question is § 4's last paragraph: whether making exactness
opt-in — authored by mapping, and authored by `panel()` — is the right
resolution, or whether an unmapped `many` port should also be made exact. This
document says the former, because the measurement shows the transitive gather
is load-bearing nowhere and the documented semantics can therefore be kept for
free.
