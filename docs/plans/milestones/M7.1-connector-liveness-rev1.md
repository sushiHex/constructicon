# M7.1 — connector liveness rev 1

**Status:** review draft. Proposes a **documentation-only** closure; the one
code change this work found is scoped here and deferred to its own slice

**Planning base:** `83ee65d` (`main` after PRs #19, #20, and #21)

**Scope:** resolve the M7 record's open connector-liveness item. The resolution
is negative: both candidate laws are refuted, the defect that motivated the
item turns out to be a third problem distinct from the two it was filed
against, and the one rule that survives is not the small slice it appears to be.

**Authority:** `docs/INVARIANTS.md` → `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` → accepted ADRs →
code/tests → this draft

Planned rather than patched because the obvious rule is wrong, the less obvious
rule is also wrong, and each is wrong for a reason worth recording once so it is
not proposed a third time.

## 1. The defect

The [M7 implementation record](../handoffs/M7-implementation-record.md) states
it as an open IR item:

> Connector liveness is not an admission rule. A connection whose source node
> contributes no resolved binding anywhere beneath the edge is admitted, so a
> member whose stable version later changes contract is silently absent from a
> `many` gather; a fault for that would close this for every graph and is an
> IR decision.

**Reproduced.** With `sdk/panel-yes` registered and promoted to emit
`sdk/Verdict` where the authored panel expects `sdk/Vote`, admission succeeds
and the aggregator's gather holds one member where two were connected. The
`PanelResult` is internally consistent and truthfully reports the members it
saw. Nothing anywhere reports the member it did not.

Two mechanisms, both in `runtime/validator.py`. `_upstream_closure` turns
connections into a per-node transitive upstream set and `_compile_graph` builds
each node's pool from every output in it, so nothing asks whether a given
connection contributed a source that was selected; and `_upstream_closure`
drops any connection whose `src` or `dst` is not a node of the graph
(`validator.py:1128`) without a fault.

## 2. The general law, refuted

The record proposes: *a connection's source must contribute a resolved binding
somewhere beneath its destination*.

An argument for it exists but is weaker than it first looks. Dead connections
do not affect compiled bindings, and they do not affect scheduling — the walker
derives dependency order from binding sources alone (`walker.py:1331`). They
are **not** without consequence, however: `ExecutionManifest` retains the whole
`source_graph`, `source_graph_hash_for` hashes it including `connections`
(`core/manifest.py:237`), that hash participates in `manifest_hash`, and effect
idempotency keys derive from `(manifest_hash, path, kind, subject)`
(`ARCHITECTURE.md:152`). A dead connection is inert in execution and load-bearing
in identity.

The law was shadow-instrumented and the suite run under it, in two variants:
*strict* (the source's own outputs must bind at the destination) and *reach*
(the source's or any of its upstream's may). Four distinct edges in graphs that
admit today are refused.

| Graph | Edge | Strict | Reach | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `issue-to-summary` | `announce → summarize` | dead | live | The M1 vertical slice, in 218 of 234 findings |
| `architect-selection` | `right → select` | dead | dead | Residue of an ambiguity repair |
| `mapped-loop` | `right → repeat` | dead | dead | Residue of an ambiguity repair |
| `sdk/wider` | `helper → panel_yes` | dead | dead | A dead edge built on purpose |

**`issue-to-summary` is established compiler behavior, not a fixture's
accident.** `tests/conftest.py:217` builds `triage → announce → summarize`
where `announce` emits `ANNOUNCED` and `summarize` consumes `BRIEF`, and
`tests/runtime/test_validator.py:20` asserts that `summarize.brief` resolves
directly from `triage.brief` through transitive scope. A connection here means
*the destination may see what the source can see* — visibility, not a wire, and
not ordering either: `announce` and `summarize` may run in parallel.

**Ambiguity repair produces dead edges by construction.** Where two nodes offer
one contract to one `one` port, admission raises `GRAPH_PORT_AMBIGUOUS` and
publishes a `Connection.map` repair naming one candidate; the loser's
connection then binds nothing. `architect-selection` is the e2e proof of that
repair loop and `mapped-loop` its unit equivalent. A law that invalidates the
repair its own fault recommends is not a law.

**`sdk/wider` builds a dead edge deliberately**, to demonstrate that a node
upstream of a member lands in the aggregator's closure and is gathered.

Reach survives only `issue-to-summary`. Neither variant is adoptable.

## 3. The gather-scoped law, also refuted

Since `many` looked like the only place silence was possible, the retreat was
to scope liveness to gathers: *when a node declares a `many` input port, every
connection into it must bind at least one output of its own source there*.
Measured over the corpus and its controls in one instrumented run, it refuses
nothing — 1359 tests, zero corpus findings, three control findings on exactly
the intended edges.

**That zero is a fact about the corpus, not about the law.** Cross-review
supplied a counterexample the corpus does not contain:

```text
C --T--> B --U--> G          G declares a `many T` input
```

`_upstream_closure` places both `C` and `B` beneath `G`; `G`'s pool takes every
output in that closure; the `many T` port gathers `C.T`. `B.U` need match
nothing. This is the `issue-to-summary` conduit with a gather at the end, and
the law refuses `B → G`. The draft claim that "at a gather there is no conduit
idiom to protect" was wrong: a conduit need not pass the gathered contract
through, only bring a producer of it into scope.

This is not a corner. `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` states it as the design:

> the gather is the general connector law: a graph input sits in every node's
> pool, and a compatible graph input or a compatible helper upstream of a
> member would widen it.

The same counterexample applies to a Loop whose body declares a `many` input
and to a composite whose declared boundary does, since both bind that boundary
from the parent pool. Three further false refusals:

- **`Connection.map` is detached from its connection's `src`.** Maps merge by
  destination and port (`validator.py:1179`) and the selector names its own
  producer, which `_resolve_selector` looks up in `node_lookup` rather than the
  pool (`validator.py:1022`). So `A → G` carrying `map={"items": "B.out"}`
  validly binds `B.out` while the law refuses `A → G`; and where `A → G` and
  `B → G` both exist and a map selects `A.out`, the law refuses the
  deliberately unselected `B` edge.
- **An optional composite output may resolve to no source**, so a composite
  connected to a gather fed compatibly elsewhere admits today and would be
  refused. `panel()` refuses such members at authoring
  (`tests/sdk/test_combinators.py:541`); a hand-authored graph may hold one.
- **Duplicate connections are legal and indistinguishable.** Closure
  construction deduplicates the parent, so the law can neither tell one
  connection from two nor say anything about multiplicity.

### The cardinality argument was also false

The draft rested on a claim that `many` is the only cardinality whose resolved
arity is unstated. The actual branch structure (`validator.py:957`):

| Cardinality | Zero typed | Several typed | Two exact-name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `one` | fault | **a unique exact-name match wins silently**; otherwise itemized ambiguity | fault |
| `optional` | accepted | same silent unique-name rule | fault |
| `many` | **fault** | all typed sources | n/a |

So `many` does not take "whatever matched" — zero is already a fault, and
resolution means one-or-more. And `one` and `optional` are not loud: a unique
exact-name match is selected silently from several typed candidates. Sets of
unstated size also arise outside a node's `many` input — a `many` graph output
binds all compatible node sources, one explicit selector may resolve to a whole
set, and `LoopExport.sources` can carry an entire resolved body-output set.
Connections never stated a gather's arity.

## 4. What survives, and why it is not a small slice

One rule stands on its own evidence:

> **A connection names two nodes.** A connection whose `src` or `dst` is not a
> node of the graph is refused.

Nothing on any admission path checks this: `_preflight_graph` never inspects
`graph.connections` (`runtime/authoring.py:88`), and the closure skips the edge.
No tracked test, fixture, or SDK generator authors one — `flow()` and `panel()`
both derive connections from generated node ids (`sdk/combinators.py:146`,
`:242`). And such a connection is not inert:

- **A ghost `src` with a real `dst` still applies its map.** `_explicit_maps`
  keys by destination and never checks endpoint membership
  (`validator.py:1179`); `_bind_node_inputs` then applies it
  (`validator.py:905`). It can even select a node that was never in the
  destination's pool. *Conditions:* the map key must name a real destination
  input, the selector must resolve, and no conflicting duplicate map may abort
  first; a selector naming the ghost itself fails.
- **Two mapped connections to a ghost `dst` can raise the duplicate-map
  error** — an admission failure attributed to a destination that does not
  exist. *Conditions:* same ghost destination and port key with two *different*
  selectors; equal selectors coalesce silently (`validator.py:1183`).
- **A mapless ghost changes `manifest_hash`** while leaving
  `resolved_connections` identical, and therefore changes every effect
  idempotency key in the run. Verified against a panel graph. *Not
  ghost-specific:* any duplicate or semantically dead legal edge does the same.

**But it cannot be shipped as a small corrective slice**, for two reasons found
in review:

1. **The fault cannot name its coordinate yet.** No new `AdmissionCode` may be
   added — the code set is the versioned wire schema, `AdmissionRejected` is
   fixed at schema 1, and the generated schema feeds
   `SystemDescription.description_digest` (`api/introspection.py:115`). The M7
   record already caught and reverted exactly this mistake once. So the fault
   goes under `graph.contract.invalid` with framed details — and
   `component + version + connection_index` is *not* an exact coordinate. A
   fault raised while compiling a referenced composite is classified against
   the root proposal (`runtime/authoring.py:80`) while bodies recurse
   (`validator.py:337`), and the frame must also address inline nested Graphs,
   inline Loop bodies, nested Graphs inside a retained composite, and several
   internal graphs whose connections each have index zero. Rendered scope
   cannot substitute: the parser states it is non-injective
   (`runtime/authoring.py:480`). The frame needs an exact root
   `AdmissionFault.path` for proposal-owned graphs, or
   `{component, version, definition_path, connection_index}` for retained ones.

2. **It changes a control contract.** `runs_counterfactual` re-admits the
   retained `source_graph` and converts every `AdmissionError` into
   `COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH` (`api/_control_commands.py:1772`), whose
   repair — "use a contract-compatible exact override" — would be wrong advice
   for this defect. Resume and reproduce are unaffected; both load the retained
   manifest and never re-admit (`walker.py:1188`). Reporting the true fault
   means admitting `AdmissionRejected` and `_AdmissionRejectPlan` from
   counterfactual, which today are accepted only for `runs_start`, changing the
   facade, the MCP tool schema, the durable-plan operation matrix, and replay
   tests. Exempting lock-driven re-admission instead is **unsafe**:
   `Constructicon.validate()` publicly accepts a `ResolutionLock`
   (`api/system.py:307`) and `admit()` never authenticates
   `source_manifest_hash`, so keying on `resolution_lock is not None` is a
   bypass. A third route — prove the graph and hash are exactly a retained
   manifest's and grandfather only that manifest's ghost endpoints — avoids the
   union change but must be stated as an explicit narrowing.

So this rule belongs in its own slice, scoped as compatibility and
control-contract hardening, not smuggled into an M7 closure.

## 5. The membership defect is a third problem

The draft re-pointed § 1 at the M7 record's other open item, kernel-attested
source identity on `many` ports. That was wrong, and the distinction matters:

- **Kernel-attested source identity is a runtime-provenance problem.** The
  manifest knows the actual sources; the walker hands a `many` port payloads in
  sealed source order and drops the source address (`walker.py:1901`), so a
  component cannot prove who sent what. The M7 record says exactly this at
  line 738, and `ARCHITECTURE.md:363` records the caveat.
- **§ 1 is an author-time problem.** The expected member disappears *before*
  the manifest is compiled. Attesting the sources that did bind cannot identify
  a member that never bound.

They must stay separate open items. And the reason § 1 is hard is now precise:
`ARCHITECTURE.md:337` says the panel's exactness is "proved at authoring from
bundles' declared contracts", and `panel()` then emits a Graph carrying no trace
of what was proved. A panel Graph does enumerate member seats as incoming
connections (`sdk/combinators.py:242`), but nothing marks those edges as
membership assertions for a particular `many` port — and reinterpreting them
that way recreates § 3's conduit counterexample exactly. The graph and manifest
do not contain enough to re-prove intended membership.

Two candidate directions, neither costed here:

1. **Durable authored membership.** An immutable expectation recorded in
   authored intent and re-proved at admission. `ResolvedPortBinding.sources`
   already records the complete *actual* admitted set (`core/manifest.py:53`),
   so a manifest change alone cannot recover the baseline — the expectation has
   to be authored. Compatible with I10 and I12.
2. **Boundary-stable promotion.** The defect only ever arises from a promotion
   that changes a *boundary*. A per-reference rule is not coherent: a bare
   `Ref` carries no expected boundary (`core/graph.py:22`) and `rdeps()` keeps
   none (`runtime/registry.py:955`). The coherent forms are to forbid
   boundary-changing stable-pointer moves globally — a major narrowing of I10's
   propagation, including rollback — or to introduce an explicit
   contract-pinned versionless reference, which is a Graph schema change and no
   longer a bare ref.

## 6. What this slice delivers

Documentation only. No code, no schema, no enum member.

- **Split the M7 record's open items three ways**, rather than merging them:
  general and gather-scoped connector liveness are *decided against*, with § 2
  and § 3 as the reason; kernel-attested runtime source provenance stays open
  unchanged; and durable authored membership is added as a separate unresolved
  problem it was previously folded into.
- **Correct current-truth architecture.** `ARCHITECTURE.md:333` says the
  aggregator "gathers every member" and that exactness is proved at authoring.
  This work shows that proof is not durable across a boundary-changing
  promotion and is never re-checked at admission. Updating only the historical
  M7 record would leave the architecture document asserting something now known
  to be conditional.
- **Retain the measurement**, in
  [`research/m7.1-connector-liveness-measurement.md`](../../../research/m7.1-connector-liveness-measurement.md),
  including the two methodology defects found and corrected mid-flight, so the
  counts here are reproducible rather than asserted.

## 7. Deferred slice — connection endpoint validation

Scoped here so the next session does not rediscover it. Not approved by this
document.

Required failure proof, if taken up:

- a ghost endpoint is refused at the root, in an inline nested Graph, in an
  inline Loop body, and in a nested Graph inside a retained composite, each
  carrying a coordinate that identifies the owning graph exactly;
- both endpoints unknown at once, and `src == dst == "ghost"`, yield one fault
  whose `missing` is a deterministically ordered list;
- a ghost `src` whose map determines a binding is refused rather than applied;
- two mapped connections to a ghost `dst` no longer raise a duplicate-map error
  attributed to a nonexistent destination;
- the defect is proved against the exported `runtime.admit` directly, not only
  through the authoring preflight;
- a retained manifest carrying a ghost still parses, resumes, and reproduces;
- counterfactual replay reports the connection fault rather than
  `COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH`, with the response-union change costed, or the
  narrowing in § 4 stated and proved;
- a pre-change golden shows an unaffected graph's manifest is byte-identical.
