# M7.1 — connector liveness rev 2

**Status:** review draft; supersedes
[rev 1](M7.1-connector-liveness-rev1.md), which is preserved and wrong in its
central conclusion

**Planning base:** `83ee65d` (`main` after PRs #19, #20, and #21)

**Scope:** close the M7 record's open connector-liveness item with two
admission laws — a `many` port binds what was connected to it, and a connection
names two nodes

**Authority:** `docs/INVARIANTS.md` → `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` → accepted ADRs →
code/tests → this draft

## 1. What rev 1 got wrong

Rev 1 concluded that no admission-time law could close the membership defect,
and proposed a documentation-only closure. That conclusion was reached without
the measurement that refutes it.

| Rev 1 said | Why it could not stand | Rev 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No connection law can close membership | Untested: rev 1 never measured *where gathered sources come from* | Scoping the gather's pool closes it |
| "At a gather there is no conduit idiom" | False under today's transitive pool | True once the pool is scoped |
| A dead edge is inert in execution | An edge nonbinding at its destination still feeds downstream through the closure | Only a removal-equivalent edge is inert, and even that changes identity |
| The defect arises from a later promotion | `panel()` emits unversioned refs, so a bundle can disagree with the *already current* stable on first admission | Timing is irrelevant; the law is about the pool |
| No authoring surface can emit a ghost connection | `admit_graph()` publicly accepts architect JSON | The endpoint law is raised, not deferred |
| Truthful counterfactual reporting needs a new response union | `ControlRejected` already carries structured details | Projected into the existing family |

Rev 1's two refutations stand and are not re-argued here: general connector
liveness (§ 2 of rev 1) and strict gather liveness over an unscoped pool (§ 3)
are both dead. This revision changes the pool instead of the rule.

## 2. The defect

A member whose resolved output contract differs from the one the panel was
authored against is silently absent from the gather. **Reproduced:** with
`sdk/panel-yes` resolving to a version that emits `sdk/Verdict` where the
authored panel expects `sdk/Vote`, admission succeeds and the aggregator's
gather holds one member where two were connected. Nothing reports the absence.

It does not require a promotion after authoring. `panel()` emits unversioned
member and aggregator refs (`sdk/combinators.py:218`) and `DefinitionBundle.ref()`
defaults to `version=None` (`sdk/types.py:47`), so a caller authoring from a
retained bundle whose same-named stable version already carries another
boundary hits it on first admission.

validator was instrumented to classify each gathered source as **direct** (its
producer has a `Connection` straight into the gathering node), **transitive**
(reachable only through the upstream closure), or **input** (a graph input,
which sits in every node's pool). Full suite, 1356 tests.

The corpus already conforms to the law below. What it does not conform to is
the law's *absence*.

## 4. The laws

> **1. A gather binds what the author named.** A `many` input port binds the
> outputs of the nodes connected directly to its node, plus any source an
> explicit `map` selects. Transitive ancestors and unnamed graph inputs do not
> widen it.
>
> **2. Every connection into a gather contributes.** A node connected to a
> gathering node that supplies nothing to it is a member that is not there, and
> admission refuses.
>
> **3. A connection names two nodes.** A connection whose `src` or `dst` is not
> a node of the graph is refused rather than skipped.

### Why scoping is the right change and not a patch

Law 1 *removes* a special case rather than adding one, and it corrects the
cardinality argument rev 1 got backwards.

For `one` and `optional`, the pool is a **search space**: exactly one source is
chosen from it, disambiguated by nominal contract, then exact port name, then
an explicit `map`, with ambiguity itemized rather than guessed. A wide pool is
a convenience, and the transitive chain in `issue-to-summary` is exactly that
convenience being used deliberately. **Those cardinalities are untouched.**

For `many`, the pool **is the answer**: every candidate in it becomes a member.
A wide pool is therefore not convenience but unbounded membership, decided by
whatever happens to be nominally compatible somewhere upstream. That is why
`panel()` needs an authoring check that "no boundary input, the request
included, carries that contract" (`ARCHITECTURE.md`), and why `sdk/wider`
exists to document the surprise. Law 1 makes the structure enforce what those
checks work around.

With the pool scoped, law 2 has no conduit counterexample left. In
`C --T--> B --U--> G`, `C.T` is no longer in `G`'s pool at all, so there is
nothing for the edge `B → G` to be spuriously credited with. Rev 1's refutation
of gather liveness was a refutation of gather liveness *over an unscoped pool*.

Law 2 is satisfied by a source binding at **any** declared input of the
gathering node, not only the `many` one, so an aggregator taking its quorum
from a connected producer is unaffected.

### What law 1 costs

- **A graph input can no longer seed a gather implicitly.** It can still do so
  explicitly, through a `map` naming `$input.<port>`, which is the author
  stating membership rather than inheriting it. `sdk/wider`'s `$input` case
  becomes an explicit map or an identity node.
- **`sdk/wider`'s assertions invert.** That test documents a hazard; under law
  1 the hazard does not exist, and the test becomes its proof.
- **`ARCHITECTURE.md`** — "the gather is the general connector law: a graph
  input sits in every node's pool, and a compatible graph input or a compatible
  helper upstream of a member would widen it" — is rewritten, not caveated.

## 5. What the laws close

- **Membership is exact.** After law 1 the gathered set is the connected set
  intersected with contract compatibility; after law 2 those are equal or
  admission refuses. The § 2 defect is closed for both the promotion case and
  the first-admission case, because neither law mentions timing.
- **`panel()`'s authoring proof becomes durable.** Its exactness is currently
  proved at authoring and never re-proved. Under law 1 the emitted topology
  *is* the membership, so admission re-proves it by construction — no authored
  expectation, no new schema, no new fault code.
- **A ghost connection stops being silent.** It is not inert today: its `map`
  still applies (`validator.py:1179`), it can raise a duplicate-map error
  attributed to a node that does not exist, and it changes `manifest_hash` and
  therefore every effect idempotency key (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, "Idempotency keys derive from `(manifest_hash, path, kind, subject)`",
  `core/effect.py:353`).

### What they do not close

- **Nominal drift is still nominal.** A member whose contract changes while
  staying nominally identical is not visible to admission.
- **Aggregator drift is not addressed.** Law 1 constrains what reaches a
  gather, not what the aggregator declares. An aggregator whose own stable
  version changes its `many` port's contract is a different failure.
- **Runtime source provenance is untouched.** The walker still drops source
  addresses when it collects a `many` port (`walker.py:1901`), so a component
  still cannot prove who sent what. That is the separate kernel-attestation
  item and this does not stand in for it.
- **An optional composite output that resolves to nothing**, connected to a
  gather, is now refused. Accepted narrowing: under law 1 that connection is a
  membership claim the composite does not honour.

## 6. Fault contract

**No new `AdmissionCode`.** The code set is the versioned wire schema,
`AdmissionRejected` is fixed at schema 1, and the generated schema feeds
`SystemDescription.description_digest` (`api/introspection.py:115`). The M7
record already caught and reverted this exact mistake once. Both faults go
under `graph.contract.invalid` with framed details, following the
retained-composite precedent from PR #21.

`runtime/authoring.py` recognizes exactly one frame today
(`carried.get("defect") == "retained_composite"`). That predicate becomes a
table keyed by `defect` — exact dispatch, never substring.

The frame must carry an **exact coordinate**. A fault raised while compiling a
referenced composite is classified against the root proposal
(`runtime/authoring.py:81`) while bodies recurse (`validator.py:337`), and
rendered scope cannot substitute because the parser states it is non-injective
(`runtime/authoring.py:480`). So `component + version + connection_index` is
insufficient — it does not say which internal Graph owns the connection, and
inline nested Graphs and inline Loop bodies have no component at all. The frame
carries either an exact root `AdmissionFault.path` for proposal-owned graphs,
or `{component, version, definition_path, connection_index}` for retained ones.

`missing`, on the endpoint fault, is a deterministically ordered list of
endpoint roles: both may be unknown at once, and `src == dst` is legal.

## 7. Compatibility

| Contract | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Graph IR | stays schema 1; no new field or construct |
| ExecutionManifest | unchanged; no new field |
| `AdmissionCode` | unchanged; no new member |
| Admission fault model | stays schema 1 |
| `description_digest` | unchanged |
| SQLite | unchanged |
| Manifest identity | byte-identical for every conforming graph |

**Counterfactual replay is affected.** `runs_counterfactual` re-admits the
retained `source_graph` and converts every `AdmissionError` into
`COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH` (`api/_control_commands.py:1772`), whose repair —
"use a contract-compatible exact override" — is wrong advice for either new
fault. Resume and reproduce are unaffected; both load the retained manifest and
never re-admit (`walker.py:1188`).

The fix needs no response-union change: counterfactual already returns
`RunSubmission | ControlRejected`, and `ControlRejected` carries structured
details with `REQUEST_INVALID` available (`core/control.py:165`). The admission
fault is projected into that existing family with an accurate repair. Behavior
and replay tests change; the facade and MCP schema do not.

Exempting lock-driven re-admission instead is **unsafe**:
`Constructicon.validate()` publicly accepts a `ResolutionLock`
(`api/system.py:307`) and `admit()` never authenticates `source_manifest_hash`,
so keying on `resolution_lock is not None` is a bypass.

## 8. Required failure proof

**Law 1 — scoping**

- a graph input nominally compatible with a gather is **not** gathered, and the
  same graph with an explicit `map` naming `$input.<port>` **is**;
- a compatible helper upstream of a member is not gathered — `sdk/wider`
  inverted, with its hazard comment replaced by the law that removes it;
- a gather left with zero sources by scoping raises the existing
  missing-source fault, not a new one;
- `one` and `optional` binding is unchanged: `issue-to-summary` admits to a
  byte-identical manifest, proved against a pre-change golden;
- scoping applies at a composite's declared `many` boundary and at a Loop
  body's `many` input, not only at an atomic node;
- a `many` **graph output** still binds from the level's node pool, since a
  graph output has no incoming connection.

**Law 2 — membership**

- the § 2 drifted-member panel is refused, and the fault names the member and
  the gather's contract;
- the same panel undrifted admits, byte-identical to its pre-change manifest;
- a node connected to a gathering node to supply a different declared input —
  an aggregator's quorum — is not refused;
- a member reachable only through its own upstream is refused, since scoping
  removed the path that made it look live.

**Law 3 — endpoints**

- a ghost endpoint is refused at the root, in an inline nested Graph, in an
  inline Loop body, and in a nested Graph inside a retained composite, each
  carrying a coordinate identifying the owning graph exactly;
- both endpoints unknown at once, and `src == dst`, yield one fault whose
  `missing` is a deterministically ordered list;
- a ghost `src` whose `map` determines a binding is refused rather than applied;
- two mapped connections to a ghost `dst` no longer raise a duplicate-map error
  attributed to a nonexistent destination;
- proved against the exported `runtime.admit` directly, and through
  `admit_graph()`'s public JSON surface, not only the authoring preflight.

**Across all three**

- counterfactual replay of a retained graph that the new laws refuse reports
  the true fault through `ControlRejected`, not `COUNTERFACTUAL_LOCK_MISMATCH`;
- a retained manifest carrying a ghost still parses, resumes, and reproduces;
- the frame table dispatches exactly, and a retained-composite fault still
  classifies as it does today.

## 10. Implementation sequence

- **PR A — scoping and membership.** Laws 1 and 2, the `sdk/wider` inversion,
  the pre-change goldens, and the architecture corrections they make necessary.
- **PR B — endpoints and the counterfactual projection.** Law 3, the frame
  table with exact coordinates, and the `ControlRejected` projection.

An **ADR is required** before PR A. "Connector liveness is decided against, and
a `many` port is scoped to what was connected" is durable IR adjudication, and
this repository puts adjudication and rationale in ADRs; a review-draft plan is
not sufficient authority for a permanent decision about the connector law.
